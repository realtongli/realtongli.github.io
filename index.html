About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Southern Queensland (UniSQ), Australia, supervised by Yan Li and Wencheng Yang. My Ph.D. is funded by the China Scholarship Council (CSC) and the Australian Research Council (ARC).

My research focuses on network security and AI security, in particular zero-day API attack detection, intrusion detection, and keeping deployed detection models up to date through runtime hot patching. I build on capsule networks, generative adversarial networks and data augmentation, and on combining domain knowledge with data-driven models. My earlier work addressed intelligent fault diagnosis of power transformers based on dissolved gas analysis (DGA).

Before joining UniSQ, I received my M.S. in Artificial Intelligence and B.Eng. in Electrical Engineering and Automation from North China Electric Power University (NCEPU), where I was supervised by Xin Shi.

I serve as a reviewer for IEEE TDSC, IEEE Communications Magazine and IEEE TNSM, and I am open to research collaborations and to academic and industry opportunities in cybersecurity and trustworthy AI.

Research Interests

  • Network Security: zero-day API attack detection, intrusion detection, attack traffic modeling
  • AI Security: runtime updating and hot patching of detection models, robustness, continual evolution
  • Deep Learning Methods: capsule networks, generative adversarial networks, data augmentation, knowledge–data fusion
  • Earlier work: DGA-based intelligent fault diagnosis of power transformers
